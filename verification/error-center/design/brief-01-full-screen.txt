A high-fidelity UI design mockup of a desktop web app screen, 1536x1024, flat and crisp like a Figma frame — a real product screenshot, not a concept poster. Light theme. This is the "Errors" tab inside the Admin area of "Lybi", a platform where teams build AI chat agents. The reference image shows the CURRENT screen: keep its visual language exactly (white panels, very light grey #f9fafb page background, thin #e5e7eb borders, 10px rounded corners, Inter-like sans-serif, small uppercase grey section labels, indigo #4f46e5 as the only accent, soft status pills) and keep the left Admin menu rail as it is (menu items with small icons; "Errors" selected with a small red count badge "7"). Redesign everything to the right of the menu so it is as convenient as possible to READ and to ACT on.

Who uses it: the CTO and a small team, several times a day, wanting answers in seconds: "Is anything broken right now? Is it hurting real customers? What exactly happened? Mark it handled."

Design the layout so that:
1. A slim summary strip across the top of the content area answers "how bad is it right now": clickable counters as filter pills — "Needs attention 7", "Looking at it 1", "Handled 2", "Ignored 0" — plus "Customer conversations hit: 3 (24h)" and a tiny 14-day bar sparkline of errors per day. One row, compact.
2. Below it, a two-pane layout. LEFT (about 360px): the list of error groups, one card per problem, sorted with the most urgent on top. Each card: a short readable title on max two lines (e.g. "Agent has no starting crew — nothing to run"), a one-line meta row with small chips for WHERE ("Whole turn", "Add-on · Profile extractor", "Model call · gemini-2.5-flash") and WHO it hit (a small green "Customer" chip, indigo "Builder", amber "Simulation"), and on the right side the count "×6" and "22 min ago". A red left edge marks problems that hit customers; a yellow "Back" pill marks a problem that was handled and returned. Above the list: one search box and a single "Filters" button — not a wall of dropdowns.
3. RIGHT: the selected problem. Header: the title, a status segmented control (New | Looking at it | Handled | Ignored) always visible in the same place, and a small "Note" button. Under it a plain-language "What happened" card in one sentence, e.g. "A whole turn failed 6 times in 6 conversations (2 customers, 1 builder, 1 simulation) since Oct 21. Last time 22 minutes ago." Next to it three small fact tiles: Kind "Our code", First seen, Last seen. Then a small timeline chart of occurrences over the last 14 days. Then "Occurrences" as a compact table (time · who · conversation #4461 · a "Open conversation" link), and one row selected showing its conversation preview (user bubble "hi", assistant failed) in a panel below. Technical details (stack trace) are tucked into a collapsed one-line "Technical details" row with a chevron, not shown open.

Rules: calm, dense but breathable, no decorative illustrations, no gradients, no dark mode, no icons bigger than 16px, red only for things that hit customers, every text in clean English and legible, realistic data. Nothing playful. It must look like the same product as the reference.
