# Otto — the palette

One thing, decided in principle. I am not asking whether; I am asking you to design it.

## 1. Give me the palette. One. Not options.

The tool is on `#6D28D9` violet, inherited from the Intelligence Center. You have twice said
keep it, with the diagnosis that the problem is violet used as atmosphere rather than the hue
itself. I applied that in full — violet is now only primary actions, links, focus, checked
controls and the current selection; the ground is `#F7F8FA` and text `#171A21`.

The founder's response, and it is final:

> **"Purple doesn't make sense. I want a colour palette that relates to what we do here."**

He does not want three options to choose between. He wants **one palette, chosen for meaning**,
and he wants it to come from what this product actually is.

**So derive it from the subject, and say out loud what you derived it from.** What this
product is:

- A tool for **retail chains and distributors** — stores, stockrooms, distribution centres,
  back offices.
- The work is **operational**: what is below safety stock, what to order and when, what sold
  where, which supplier is late. Inventory, purchasing, sales, branches, suppliers.
- The people are **not developers**. A purchasing manager, a branch operations lead, a
  logistics person. They currently run their week off spreadsheets and reports somebody else
  made for them.
- What Otto does is **construct** — the user describes a screen and Otto builds it. He is the
  technical colleague they never had.
- The product's deepest claim is that it is **checkable**: every number traces to real data,
  nothing is invented. Nothing in the palette should feel speculative, magical or synthetic.

The founder's bar: this has to feel like a **"wow" system for SMB retailers** — technical and
credible, not enterprise-grey and not consumer-AI.

**Deliver:**

- The thesis in one or two sentences: what this palette is derived from, and what it says.
- The complete token set with hex: canvas, surface, subtle surface, hover, disabled, text,
  secondary text, placeholder, border, strong border, control border, accent, accent hover,
  accent active, accent subtle, accent border. Then success / warning / danger / info as
  text + background + border triples, a neutral status triple, and six chart series with the
  first series NOT being the accent.
- Contrast ratios for every pair that carries text, checked rather than assumed.
- What this palette is worse at. Every real choice costs something; name it.
- Where the accent is allowed and where it is banned — you already wrote this rule for violet
  and it was the most useful thing in the last answer. Rewrite it for the new accent.

**The one real complication, which you must solve rather than avoid:** a screen built in Otto
is opened later inside the Intelligence Center, which is violet today. Tell me how to handle
that. Does the Intelligence Center adopt the new palette, does Otto's generated output carry
a neutral theme that sits acceptably in both, or is a visible split fine and why? Give the
recommendation and the migration, not the trade-off lecture.


## Constraints

Hebrew RTL throughout. Free or already-licensed assets only. No emoji. Operational software
used for forty minutes at a stretch, by people under time pressure. The built screen is the
hero and nothing may compete with it. Check contrast before specifying a pair.

## Output

The palette, complete and final: thesis, token set, contrast table, what it is worse at, and
the accent-usage rule. Nothing else — do not design layout in this answer.
